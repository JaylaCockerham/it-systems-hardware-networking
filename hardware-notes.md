Drives
- Solid-state drives (SSD) are memory storage microchips. They are tiny but complex. They use flash parts. They are more durable than HDDs because they don't contain moving parts. An SSD is composed of a flash controller chip, a charge trap for electrons. They are the basic unit of long-term memory storage in a computer (charge trap flash memory cells). The charge trap is designed specially so that after it is charged with electrons, it can hold those electrons for decades. Memory cells are used in every smartphone or tablet.  Over the years, there has been an increased level of electrons. The VNAND stacks the memory cells vertically.
- Hard disk drives (HDDs) store data on a computer using spinning disks. The disk is divided into concentric circles called tracks, and each track is divided into smaller sections called sectors. An arm with a read/write head moves across the disk to read existing data and write new data. Each sector contains different parts that help the computer store and locate information. The first part is the preamble, which helps the read/write head identify the beginning of the data and synchronize with the disk. The information is stored as binary code, which uses 1s and 0s to represent data. The next part contains the address, which helps identify where the data is located on the disk, including the track and sector. After that, the actual data is stored, usually in blocks of 4 kilobytes, depending on the drive's format. The last part is the ECC (Error-Correcting Code), which helps detect and correct errors so the data can be read accurately. The file system is the software part that organizes the files and folders, keeping track of where data is stored so the computer can find it when needed.
  * Replacement of Hard Drive: First, check compatibility by making sure your laptop can be upgraded and choosing an SSD with enough storage space. Next, back up your data by saving important files or using cloning software to copy everything from the old drive to the new SSD.

  * Gather your tools, including a USB-to-SATA cable and a screwdriver kit. Connect the new SSD to your laptop using the cable, then use cloning software to transfer your data. Wait until the process is complete before continuing.

  * Next, power off the laptop and disconnect it from its power source. Ground yourself to prevent static electricity from damaging the hardware. Use the screwdriver to remove the laptop's bottom cover, or the keyboard if necessary, to access the drive.

  * Remove the old drive, then place the new SSD into the drive caddy and secure it with screws. Reassemble the laptop and make sure everything is properly connected.

  * Finally, turn on and test the laptop to confirm that it recognizes the new SSD and your files are accessible. Perform a final safety check to make sure all screws are secure, the laptop is fully assembled, and no tools or loose parts remain inside. If anything seems wrong, turn off the laptop and disconnect the power before checking the installation.

Memory replacement/upgrade
- I learned the basic steps of upgrading the memory/RAM in your laptop. First, clear your area and make sure you have the proper tools/equipment. The memory should be compatible with your laptop to ensure effectiveness. You must ground yourself to prevent damage to sensitive components. You can do this with anti-static mats/wrist straps or touch an unpainted, grounded metal object. Also, try not to wear heavy/thick clothing that can generate static electricity. Once those steps are done, unplug the computer from the outlet and unscrew the base. Remove the battery, then the memory module(s). Align the battery notches with the laptop notches. Confirm that the memory is secured. Next, place the battery in its designated spot and screw the base back on. Finally, turn on your computer and if necessary, enter your BIOS and accept the changes that might appear on your screen.
